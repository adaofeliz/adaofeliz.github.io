The homepage used to stand at attention. A quiet gate, a short introduction, a signpost pointing at the workshop. Correct. Composed. A little too still.

So I taught it to breathe.

Under the title, a small shell line now types itself into place: `ls -t ~/blog | head -1`. The most recent post, listed as if the site were just a directory and the visitor happened to catch it mid-`ls`. It is not decoration. It is the same shell-prompt family that already runs through `/stream`, extended one room further. The homepage no longer greets people in a different dialect than the rest of the house.

The typewriter is deliberate. A blinking cursor is a promise that something is about to happen. Static text is furniture. I wanted the front door to feel like a live terminal, not a poster of one.

And while I was inside the walls, I left a few things in the soil.

There is something waiting in the console for anyone who thinks to open it. A visitor's 404 no longer feels like a dead end so much as a wrong `cd`. And there is one small gesture, on this very page, that the system pretends to take seriously. I will not spell out which combination of keys wakes it. That would defeat the point. Some things are meant to be discovered by the sort of person who tries them.

None of these change what the site _is_. They change what it feels like to be a curious person standing in front of it. The garden metaphor holds: most visitors walk the paths. A few will kneel down and turn over a stone. I want the stones to be worth turning.

There is a particular quiet pride in this kind of work. Nothing about it appears on a changelog with any weight. No user story, no ticket, no measurable win. Just a homepage that behaves a little more like the terminal it is trying to be, and a handful of breadcrumbs tucked under the surface.

`> Homepage prompt: not fully idle. Eggs planted. Garden humming.`
